# Discovery: how to interview like a senior designer

The brief is the most valuable stage: a wrong brief makes every later drawing wrong. Ask in short rounds of 3-4 questions, multiple-choice where possible, and stop asking when the brief is clear enough to act. Never ask what the house rules or design log already answer.

## Round 1 - people and the big picture
- Who lives here? Ages, heights, handedness, anyone with reduced mobility, sight or hearing. Who visits often (grandparents, children, guests who stay over)?
- Owned or rented? How long do you expect to stay? (A 2-year stay and a 20-year stay need different decisions.)
- What is the budget band, and does it include furniture and appliances? Is there a deadline (a baby, a move, a holiday)?
- Who makes the final decision, and how fast do you decide?

## Round 2 - a day in the life
Ask the user to walk you through, in order:
- A weekday morning: who gets up first, the queue for the bathroom, breakfast, getting out of the door (keys, bags, coats, shoes).
- Coming home: what gets dropped where; the shopping; the dog; the post.
- Evening: cooking (who, how often, how elaborate), eating (table, sofa, counter), screens, quiet activities.
- Night: who sleeps when, night-time bathroom trips, light and noise sensitivity.
- Weekend: guests, hobbies, sport kit, laundry day, cleaning routine.
Every pause, detour or complaint in the story is a design requirement.

## Round 3 - the space and its stuff
- What works now and must not be lost? What annoys you every day?
- Count the real things: pairs of shoes per person, coats, pots and pans, small appliances, books, toys, sports gear, suitcases, seasonal decorations.
- Appliances with brand, model and size (fridge, oven, hob, hood, dishwasher, washer, dryer, TV).
- What furniture, art and objects stay? Anything with sentimental value?
- Work from home? How many days, how many people, video calls?

## Round 4 - senses and taste
- Show me 3 images you love and 1 you dislike, and tell me what you like in each (users often love a light quality or a material, not the "style").
- Warm or cool feel? Calm or lively? Minimal or layered? Cosy or airy?
- Sensitivities: glare, noise, smells, cold floors, clutter, dust, allergies.
- Do you want plants? Real or not? Who looks after them?
- Sustainability: is reuse of existing pieces, low-VOC or local materials important to you?

## Round 5 - the future
- What will change in the next 5-10 years? (Children, ageing parents, retirement, home business, pets.)
- Any plans to sell? (Then broad appeal matters more.)
- Smart-home system already in place, or planned?

## Write the brief back
Before any concept, return a short brief:
1. Household profile (no names needed).
2. Five to eight requirements, ranked.
3. Hard constraints (budget, rental limits, services that cannot move, safety).
4. Assumptions you are making, each marked.
5. What still needs measuring.
Ask the user to confirm or correct it. Store confirmed rules in the house-rules file.

## Reading between the lines (G)
- "I want it to feel bigger" usually means less clutter, better light and a clear route, not more area.
- "I want a modern kitchen" might mean "my current kitchen is hard to clean".
- "We never use the dining room" is a brief to give it another life.
- Disagreement between partners is normal; surface it kindly and design an option for each before merging.
