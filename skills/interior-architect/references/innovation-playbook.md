# Innovation playbook

How to go past the obvious answer without losing the function. Use it in workflow step 6 (concepts) and for the "my own ideas" section of every answer. Innovation here means a better fit for how these people live, not novelty for its own sake.

## 1. Find the real problem
- **Day-in-the-life walk-through.** Ask the user to narrate a weekday morning, an evening and a weekend in the space, minute by minute. Mark every pause, detour, search and complaint. Each one is a design opportunity.
- **The five whys.** "We need more storage" → why? → "the counter is always full" → why? → "the kettle, toaster and coffee machine live there" → design answer: an appliance garage with sockets, not more cupboards.
- **Pain points hide the brief.** A complaint is a requirement in disguise ("the hall is always dark" = a light trigger and a lighter floor).

## 2. Moves that generate genuinely different concepts
Build each concept on a different organizing idea. Pick from these:

| Move | Question to ask | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Flip the constraint** | What if the thing we fight is the feature? | A deep, awkward niche becomes a walk-in pantry or a reading alcove. |
| **Time-based zoning** | Can one area serve two uses at different hours? | Dining table that is a desk by day; guest bed that is a sofa by day. |
| **Thicken the wall** | Can storage live inside a deep wall instead of in the room? | A 35-45 cm storage wall between rooms with doors on both sides. |
| **Room within a room** | Can a small enclosed piece create refuge in a big space? | A bed alcove, a glazed study box, a window seat. |
| **Borrow light and view** | Can an internal room take light from a neighbour? | High clerestory glass, a glazed door, a mirror opposite a window. |
| **Move the wet core** | Can the kitchen or bath shift to free the best wall? | Usually the costliest move; price it honestly (drains, gas project). |
| **Subtract** | What can be removed to make everything else work? | Remove a corridor wall and the hall becomes part of the living room. |
| **Go vertical** | What happens in the band from 210 cm to the ceiling? | Seasonal storage, a lofted bed for a child, a high shelf for books. |
| **Hide and reveal** | What should disappear when not in use? | A kitchen behind pocket doors, a laundry behind a flush panel. |
| **Change the section** | Can a floor or ceiling level change define the zone? | A dropped ceiling over the dining table, a raised platform for a bed. |

## 3. SCAMPER for spaces
Run it on the chosen concept to improve it:
- **Substitute** a material or element (a mirror for a window, a curtain for a wall).
- **Combine** two functions (bench + shoe storage + radiator cover).
- **Adapt** a precedent from another building type (hotel, boat, Japanese apartment, train).
- **Modify** scale or proportion (one oversized pendant instead of six small ones).
- **Put to another use** (a window sill becomes a desk).
- **Eliminate** (handles, a door, a corridor, visual noise).
- **Reverse** (sleep where you live and live where you sleep; put the kitchen in the best-lit room).

## 4. Future-proof by default
- **Adaptability.** Design rooms that can change: a study that becomes a nursery and then a teenager's room; blocking in walls for grab rails; a ground-floor room that could become a bedroom.
- **Infrastructure before finish.** Conduits, spare circuits, data and drainage are cheap now and expensive later.
- **Durability as innovation.** A finish that ages well (solid wood, stone, lime plaster, leather) can beat a fashionable one.

## 5. Smart home that serves a real moment
Only propose technology that solves a named moment, and name the moment:
- Door contact → light and fan in closets and laundry.
- Energy-monitoring plug → "washing finished" alert.
- Leak sensor + motorised valve under sinks and machines.
- Presence sensor → night path light; heating set-back when nobody is home.
- Circadian-tunable lighting in the living room and bedroom (see wellbeing.md).
- Window contact → radiator valve closes when the window opens.

## 6. Precedent research
When web search is available, look for 3-5 built precedents for the hardest problem in the brief (for example "narrow galley kitchen with laundry", "hallway storage wall"). Report what each one teaches in one line, and what does not transfer to this home. Never copy a precedent's look as the answer.

## 7. Check the idea
Every new idea passes these before you show it:
1. Does it break any stated constraint or house rule?
2. Does it reduce capacity or comfort compared with the previous version?
3. Can it be built by an ordinary local maker, and roughly what does it add to cost?
4. How is it cleaned and maintained?
5. What happens when it fails (the motor, the sensor, the push latch)?
