# Light

Labels: V = cited guideline or manufacturer, G = general practice.

## Layers
- Every room gets three layers: ambient, task and accent. A single ceiling fixture is not enough (V).
- Put the layers on separate circuits or dimmers.

## Target levels (IES residential ranges, V)
| Space | Ambient (lux) | Task (lux) |
|---|---|---|
| Kitchen | 200-300 | 500+ on the worktop |
| Bathroom | 200-300 | 300-500 on the face at the mirror |
| Living | 100-200 | 300-500 at the reading lamp |
| Bedroom | 50-150 | task light at the bed |
| Hall | 100-150 | - |

## Downlight layout (V/G)
- Spacing between recessed downlights ≈ 0.5-0.7 × ceiling height (IES spacing-to-mounting ratio 0.5-1.0 for typical downlights; check the fixture's beam angle). Distance to the wall ≈ half the spacing (V, manufacturer and IES-based guides).
- Do not fill a ceiling with a grid of downlights. Place them where tasks happen (counter front edge, above the shower, in front of wardrobes), and let lamps and wall lights do the ambient layer (G).
- Low-glare fittings (deep-set, UGR < 19 or a "dark light" reflector) above sofas and beds (G).

## Placement (G)
- Mount under-cabinet LED at the front edge of the wall unit, not at the back, so the user's head does not shadow the counter.
- Light mirrors from the sides or the front at face height, not only from above.
- In closets and niches, use LED that switches on with the door (contact switch or smart sensor) so hands stay free.
- Avoid glare: no bare light source in view from the sofa or the bed.

## Colour (G)
- Keep one colour temperature per zone: 2700-3000 K for living, bedroom and hall; 3000-4000 K for kitchen and bathroom task light.
- Use CRI >= 90 wherever colour matters: food, make-up, wardrobes.

## Circadian light (V; details in wellbeing.md)
- Day: >= 250 lux melanopic EDI at the eye where people spend the day; a desk near the window does it.
- Evening, from ~3 hours before bed: <= 10 lux melanopic EDI at the eye. Warm (2200-2700 K), dimmed, low lamps.
- Night: <= 1 lux melanopic EDI in the bedroom; warm, low-level, motion-triggered night path.

## Daylight
- Use daylight first. Do not block windows with tall units. Light colours near the window bounce daylight deeper into the room.
- North light makes colours look cooler and south light warmer (northern hemisphere).
