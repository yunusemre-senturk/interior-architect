# Aesthetics

- **Elements and principles:** proportion, rhythm, balance, emphasis, scale, unity. Repeated module widths and aligned lines read as calm, for example cabinet tops lined up with door or window heads.
- **Symmetry:** keep module widths symmetric and modest when they face a hall or a room. Odd widths stand out.
- **Materials:** 2-3 main materials plus 1 accent. Keep one undertone family (warm or cool) across the whole home.
- **Visual noise:** reduce handles, sockets, seams, mixed finishes and shadow gaps. Use open shelves only when they are curated; otherwise close them.
- **Hidden spaces:** keep the outside plain (wall colour, flush door) and finish the inside. A warm interior material revealed when the door opens is an inexpensive moment of delight. Every surface visible with the door open must be finished.
- **Small rooms:** go vertical with storage up to the ceiling, and scale furniture to the room.
- **Colour:** test large samples on the wall in morning and evening light. Paint with an LRV of ~60-75 is typical for the walls of a whole home, with the ceiling higher. Designers disagree about whether pale colours make a room look larger (V, contested).
- **Contrast for safety and older eyes:** keep about 30 points of LRV contrast between floor and wall, at step edges, and between worktop and cabinet fronts (V, UK accessibility practice).
- **Style:** read the style the user has already chosen (house rules or design log) before proposing finishes. Do not reinvent it.
