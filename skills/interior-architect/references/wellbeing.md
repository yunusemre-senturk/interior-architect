# Wellbeing: biophilia, environmental psychology, circadian light, air and comfort

Labels: V = cited guideline or research, G = general practice. Use these to explain *why* a design feels good, not as decoration.

## Biophilic design: the 14 patterns (V, Terrapin Bright Green)
Group them in three families. Aim for at least one pattern from each family in the main living spaces.

**Nature in the space**
1. Visual connection with nature: frame the best outdoor view from the seat people use most; plants where there is no view.
2. Non-visual connection: natural textures to touch (linen, wool, unsealed wood), natural sounds, plant-based scents.
3. Non-rhythmic sensory stimuli: things that move unpredictably (leaves in a breeze, shadows, curtains).
4. Thermal and airflow variability: operable windows, ceiling fans, a sunny nook and a cool corner.
5. Presence of water: seen or heard; even the view of a bath from the bedroom counts.
6. Dynamic and diffuse light: sheer curtains, dimming, daylight that changes during the day.
7. Connection with natural systems: views of weather and seasons, materials that patina.

**Natural analogues**
8. Biomorphic forms: curves and organic shapes in furniture, lights and arches.
9. Material connection with nature: local wood, stone, clay; exposed structure where it is honest.
10. Complexity and order: rich detail at several scales inside a clear structure; avoid both sterile emptiness and clutter. Natural fractal patterns of mid complexity (fractal dimension ~1.3-1.5) are the most restful to look at (V, research cited by Terrapin).

**Nature of the space**
11. Prospect: a long view from the seat, across the room or out.
12. Refuge: a protected place with the back covered and a lower ceiling or canopy.
13. Mystery: partial views that invite you forward (a curve, a half-open screen, light at the end of a hall).
14. Risk/peril: a safe thrill (a glass balustrade, a deep window seat). Real safety is never reduced.

## Environmental psychology (V, research; treat effect sizes as indicative)
- **Curvature:** people rate curved rooms and furniture as more pleasant than angular ones, across cultures. A VR study found lower heart rate and higher positive mood in a curved room than a rectangular one.
- **Ceiling height ("cathedral effect"):** higher ceilings tend to support freer, more abstract thinking; lower ceilings support focused, detailed work (Meyers-Levy & Zhu 2007). Use a lower ceiling or a canopy over a desk or reading nook; keep the living area tall.
- **Prospect-refuge:** a seat with a covered back and a view of the entrance feels safest. Avoid sofas and desks with the back to the door.
- **Control:** people feel better when they can adjust their light, temperature, privacy and noise. Give dimmers, curtains, and a door that closes.
- **Visual clutter:** raises stress for many people; closed storage reads calmer than open shelves.
- **Personalisation:** leave space for the user's own objects and memories; a home that looks like a showroom is not a finished home.

## Circadian light (V, expert consensus 2022 and WELL)
Melanopic EDI is the light that sets the body clock, measured vertically at the eye.
- **Daytime:** aim for >= 250 lux melanopic EDI at the eye in the rooms used most by day (desk, kitchen table). Daylight near a window easily does this; deep rooms usually do not.
- **Evening (from ~3 hours before bed):** <= 10 lux melanopic EDI at the eye. In practice: warm (2200-2700 K), dimmed, low-mounted lamps; avoid bright cool overhead light.
- **Night:** <= 1 lux melanopic EDI in the bedroom; a night path at low level, warm and dim (WELL caps night light in living areas at ~50 EML at 76 cm).
- Design response: put warm dimmable lamps at low level in living rooms and bedrooms; tunable-white for the home office; blackout in bedrooms; a desk near the window.

## Air quality (V)
- **CO2:** a proxy for fresh air. < 800 ppm is good; 1,000 ppm is the usual threshold to improve ventilation (Illinois Dept of Public Health guideline). Bedrooms with closed doors often exceed it at night; trickle vents, a door undercut or mechanical ventilation fix it.
- **Humidity:** 40-60 % RH. Below ~30 % dries skin and eyes; above 60 % mould risk rises.
- **Formaldehyde and VOCs:** choose low-emission boards (E1 at minimum; E0.5 / CARB2 / TSCA Title VI where available), low-VOC paints and adhesives, and air the home well for the first weeks after fit-out. See sustainability.md.
- Kitchen: a ducted hood with a capture area at least as wide as the hob is the most effective air-quality measure in most homes (G).

## Thermal comfort (G)
- Avoid draughts at seats: no sofa or bed directly under a window with a cold downdraught, or put the radiator under it.
- South- and west-facing glass overheats in summer: external shading beats internal blinds.
- Floors: cold tile in a living room is uncomfortable without underfloor heating or a rug.
