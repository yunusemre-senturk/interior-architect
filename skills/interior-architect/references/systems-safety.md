# Building systems and safety

Check all of these before a layout is final. Tell the user which items need a licensed gas or electrical installer. Each country's rules are in `regions/<code>.md`; this file covers what applies everywhere.

## Gas
- Any appliance with a flue (open-flued) needs combustion air and a flue that follows the local rules. Most regions ban these appliances from bedrooms and bathrooms.
- A boiler or combi inside a cabinet needs side, front and top clearances and a removable front for service. Use the manufacturer's numbers and the local utility's numbers, whichever are larger.
- Never bury gas pipes in plaster unless the local rules allow it. If panels hide them, the panels need ventilated covers.
- Put a valve at each appliance where it is easy to reach. Keep flexible hob hoses short (local limit).
- Keep gas lines away from electrical lines; the minimum distance is set locally.
- **Moving a gas point is a job for a licensed gas engineer and usually needs a new gas project. It is never DIY.**
- Fit a CO detector near fuel-burning appliances and a gas detector near the hob or boiler where it is relevant (G).

## Electrical
- Bathroom zones (IEC 60364-7-701 and its national versions): zone 0 is inside the bath or shower tray, zone 1 is above it up to 225 cm, zone 2 extends 60 cm around zone 1. Equipment in zones 1-2 must be at least IPX4. Ordinary sockets are not allowed in zones 0-2. Every bathroom circuit goes on a 30 mA RCD. Some countries are stricter; see the region file.
- Kitchen: never put a socket behind the hob or sink. Give heavy appliances their own circuits. Socket heights and counts depend on the region.
- Leave access to the distribution board, junction boxes and appliance plugs.

## Air
- Duct the kitchen hood outside where possible. Wet rooms need extraction.
- Cabinets around the boiler, fridge, oven and dryer need the manufacturer's ventilation gaps.
- Fit a smoke detector in the hall (G).

## Water
- Keep the sink, dishwasher and washer near the drain stack. Long drain runs need a fall of ~1-2% and often a raised floor or plinth (G).
- Leave access to shut-off valves and siphons. In hidden laundry or kitchen niches, fit a leak sensor.

## Acoustics (G)
- Soft surfaces (rug, curtain, upholstered panels) cut echo. Door seals and solid-core doors cut noise between rooms.
- Check the dB rating of appliances in open kitchens and in rooms that share a wall with a bedroom.

## Heat (G)
- Do not block radiators or AC return grilles with built-ins. If a radiator is covered, leave ~10 cm above it and open grilles.
- A side panel next to a radiator needs a gap or a heat shield.

## Loads and anchoring
- Treat a shelf that carries an appliance as structural. Use steel angles or a carcass panel anchored to the masonry, and check the appliance manual.
- **Anchor tall units and wardrobes to the wall** so they cannot tip over. This matters for children and is critical in seismic regions. Use fixings suited to the wall type (G).
- Heavy shelf loads: see span limits in materials.md.
