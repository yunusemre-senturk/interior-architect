# Project delivery: budget, sequence, quotes, site, handover

Labels: V = cited source, G = general practice. Costs are regional; never give a price as fact without a local source and date.

## Budget (V/G)
- **Contingency:** 10-15 % on most renovations, 15-20 % on homes older than ~30 years or when walls are opened (V, NAHB guidance as reported; HUD 203(k) requires ≥ 10 % on 30+ year homes). Kitchen and bath projects often overrun by 20 % or more (V, industry reporting).
- **Where the money goes (G):** in a kitchen, cabinets and worktops are typically the largest share, then appliances, then installation. In a bathroom, labour and waterproofing often exceed the fixtures.
- **Allowances are traps:** a quote with a "tile allowance" lower than the tile the user wants is a future extra. Price the real chosen product.
- **Cost bands for concepts:** label each concept low / mid / high relative to each other, and name the cost drivers (moving drains or gas, custom joinery, stone, structural work).
- **Save money without losing quality (G):** keep wet rooms where they are; use standard cabinet carcasses with custom fronts; spend on what you touch daily (handles, taps, worktop, the sofa) and save on what you do not; phase the work so infrastructure is done once.

## Order of works (V, renovation practice)
1. Design and scope locked: drawings, finish schedule, every fixture chosen, change policy in writing.
2. Permits and approvals (gas project, building notice, landlord or building management consent) filed during design.
3. Site protection and dust control.
4. Demolition: selective first to reveal conditions; order long-lead items as soon as conditions are known.
5. Structural changes and framing.
6. First fix: electrical, plumbing, gas, ventilation ducts, data, before walls close.
7. Inspections of first fix (and photos of every pipe and cable before covering; keep them for the household).
8. Insulation, boarding, plaster, screed. Waterproofing in wet rooms (and a flood test before tiling).
9. Primer and first coat of paint.
10. Floors: wet trades (tile, stone) first, then timber, vinyl, carpet.
11. Joinery, kitchen, doors, trims; second fix electrical and plumbing.
12. Final paint, snagging, final inspection, handover.

Common mistakes: getting quotes from vague ideas; ordering custom joinery at the last minute; tiling before the kitchen layout is final; painting before the dusty trades are done.

## Getting and comparing quotes (G)
- Send the same drawings and the same finish list to 2-3 makers.
- Ask each for: an itemised price, what is excluded, lead time, payment stages, warranty, who measures on site, who handles waste removal, and how changes are priced.
- Compare like for like: board thickness, edge type, hinge and runner brand, worktop thickness, installation and removal included or not.
- The cheapest quote with exclusions is often the most expensive project.
- Payment in stages tied to completed work, never most of it up front.

## Site visits: what to check (G)
- First fix: socket and switch positions and heights against the drawing, pipe positions, drain falls, boxes for wall lights.
- Before tiling: waterproofing continuity, wall flatness, tile set-out agreed on the wall.
- Before joinery: final measurements by the maker, wall plumb, floor level.
- Take dated photos at every visit.

## Change control (G)
- Every change after design lock: written, priced, and approved before it is built. Log it in the design log with its cost and delay.

## Handover (G)
- Walk every room with the snag list (assets/snag-list.template.md): finishes, alignments, doors and drawers, silicone, paint, lights and sockets working, water pressure, drainage, ventilation.
- Collect manuals, warranties, paint codes, tile batch numbers, and the photos of hidden services.
- Leave a short maintenance note per finish (how to clean, what to avoid, when to reseal or re-oil).
