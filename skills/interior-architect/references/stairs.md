# Stairs, balustrades and level changes

Labels: V = cited guideline, R = regulation, G = general practice. Numbers in cm. Codes differ by country: always check the region file and the local code.

## Comfort formula (G, Blondel)
**2 × riser + going = 60-65 cm** (the length of a normal stride). Example: riser 17.5, going 27 → 62. Keep every riser in a flight identical; people trip on a step that differs by even 1 cm.

## Typical limits (check the region)
| Item | US IRC (R) | Good practice (G) |
|---|---|---|
| Riser | ≤ 19.7 [7¾"] | 16-18 |
| Going (tread depth) | ≥ 25.4 [10"] | 26-30 |
| Variation within a flight | ≤ 0.95 [⅜"] | 0 |
| Clear width | ≥ 91 [36"] | 90-110 in a home |
| Headroom | ≥ 203 [6'8"] | ≥ 210 |
| Handrail height | 86-97 [34-38"] | one side ≥ 3-4 risers; both sides for older users |

Turkey: see `regions/tr.md`. The UK, EU countries and others have their own limits (for example a maximum pitch).

## Balustrades and guarding
- Guard any drop above ~60-76 (code dependent) with a balustrade ~90-110 high (R, varies).
- No openings that let a 10 cm sphere pass; no horizontal rails children can climb (R in most codes).
- Glass balustrades need laminated toughened glass with a specified fixing system (V, makers).

## Design notes (G)
- Winders (triangular steps) save space but are the most common place to fall. If you must use them, keep a usable going at the walking line (~30 from the narrow end).
- Light every stair: a switch at top and bottom, or step lights; contrasting nosings for older eyes.
- Under-stair space: pull-out storage, a WC (check headroom over the pan and in front), a desk, a pet nook.
- A single step inside a room is a trip hazard. Use at least two, mark them with light and contrast, or ramp it.
- Spiral stairs suit occasional use only; furniture cannot be carried up them.
