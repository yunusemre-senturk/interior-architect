# Furnishing and styling: rugs, art, curtains, colour, scale

Labels: V = cited guideline, G = general practice. Numbers in cm, with inches in brackets where the source is American.

## Rugs (G, designer practice)
- **Living room:** at least the front legs of every seat on the rug. The rug runs ~15-20 [6-8"] past the sofa on each side (some designers use 20-30). Leave 30-45 [12-18"] of floor between rug and wall in a normal room; less looks like wall-to-wall carpet, more looks stingy.
- **Dining:** the rug extends ~60-90 [24-36"] beyond the table edge on every side, so chairs stay on it when pulled out.
- **Bedroom:** ~45-60 [18-24"] of rug on the three free sides of the bed, or runners on each side. Common: 200 × 300 for a 160 bed, 250 × 350 for a 180 bed.
- **Hall runner:** leave ~10-15 of floor on each side along the length.
- Typical sizes: 160 × 230, 200 × 300, 250 × 350 [5×8, 6×9, 8×10, 9×12 ft].
- Undercuts: check that doors clear the rug and underlay.

## Art and mirrors (G)
- Centre of a single piece, or of a group, at ~145-152 [57-60"] above the floor (average eye height).
- Above a sofa or console: bottom edge ~15-25 above the furniture top; width ≈ 2/3 of the furniture width.
- Gallery wall: treat the group as one piece; 5-8 [2-3"] between frames.
- Above a bed headboard: ≥ 20 clear, and fixed securely (seismic zones: use closed hooks or security fixings, never a nail).
- Mirrors opposite windows double the daylight and the view; opposite a bed, some people find them unsettling.

## Curtains and blinds (G)
- Rod or track: at least 10-15 [4-6"] above the frame, ideally just below the ceiling or cornice (5-10 below). It makes windows look taller.
- Width: extend 15-30 [6-12"] beyond the frame on each side, so the stack does not cover glass.
- Fullness: 2-2.5 × the track width for pleated curtains, 1.5-2 for wave/S-fold.
- Length: just kissing the floor (≤ 1 cm gap) for a tailored look; a 2-5 cm break for a softer look; avoid "high-water" curtains that stop 5-10 above the floor.
- Plan a ceiling pocket for tracks and blinds before the plaster stage.

## Furniture scale and spacing (G)
See living-bedroom.md for clearances. In addition:
- Coffee table ≈ 2/3 the sofa length, height within ~5 of the seat height.
- Side table top within ~5 of the sofa arm height.
- Pendant over a dining table: bottom 75-85 above the table top; diameter ≈ 1/2-2/3 of the table width; ≥ 15 in from each table edge.
- Chandelier diameter rule of thumb: room length + width in feet = diameter in inches (≈ (L + W in m) × 8 cm).
- Bedside tables at or a few cm below mattress top height.
- TV: centre of screen near seated eye height (100-110); viewing distance about 1.2-1.6 × the screen diagonal for 4K (G).

## Colour (G)
- **60-30-10:** about 60 % dominant colour (walls, large rug), 30 % secondary (upholstery, curtains, joinery), 10 % accent (cushions, art, a door).
- **Schemes:** monochrome (one hue, many values, very calm); analogous (neighbours on the wheel, easy harmony); complementary (opposites, energetic; use one as the 10 % accent).
- **Undertone first:** decide warm (yellow/red base) or cool (blue/green base) and keep whites, woods, stones and metals in the same family.
- **LRV:** walls 60-75 for a bright home, ceiling higher; keep ~30 points of LRV contrast at floor-wall and step edges for older eyes (see aesthetics.md).
- **Daylight direction** (northern hemisphere): north rooms make colours cooler, so warm them; south rooms take cooler tones well.
- **Test** A4-size samples on two walls, morning and evening, beside the real floor.

## Textiles and texture (G)
- Mix at least three textures (smooth, nubby, soft) in one room. In a monochrome scheme, texture does the work colour would do.
- Performance fabrics (solution-dyed acrylic, high double-rub count ≥ 30,000 Martindale for daily sofas) for children and pets.
- Wool rugs resist soiling and burn slowly; viscose rugs mark with water.

## Styling (G)
- Group objects in odd numbers, with varied heights, on a tray or a book stack.
- Leave negative space; every shelf does not need an object.
- Plants: pick by the light the spot actually gets, not by the look.
