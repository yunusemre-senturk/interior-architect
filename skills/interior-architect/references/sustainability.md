# Sustainability and healthy materials

Labels: V = cited guideline or research, G = general practice.

## Why it matters in interiors (V)
Repeated fit-outs and renovations can add more embodied carbon over a building's life than the original construction (industry research summarised by JLL and others). The greenest renovation is the one that keeps most of what is there.

## Order of choices (G)
1. **Keep and repair:** existing floors, doors, kitchen carcasses, solid furniture. Refinish, reface, reupholster.
2. **Reuse:** salvaged doors, radiators, stone, vintage furniture, reclaimed timber.
3. **Design less material:** fewer layers, no decorative cladding over sound walls, standard board sizes to cut waste (2800 × 2070 or 2440 × 1220 boards).
4. **Choose low-impact new materials:** FSC/PEFC wood, recycled-content tiles, cork, linoleum, lime and clay plasters, wool.
5. **Design for disassembly:** screwed rather than glued, standard hardware, separable materials, so parts can be repaired and recycled.

## Healthy indoor air (V)
- **Boards:** formaldehyde class E1 is a minimum in the EU; prefer E0.5 or CARB Phase 2 / TSCA Title VI compliant panels.
- **Paints and adhesives:** low- or zero-VOC labels (EU Ecolabel, GREENGUARD Gold, Blue Angel, or national equivalents).
- **Ventilate after fit-out:** air the home well for 2-4 weeks; new furniture and finishes off-gas most in the first weeks (G).
- WHO and national guidelines give formaldehyde limits around 0.1 mg/m³ (≈ 0.08 ppm) for short exposure; some agencies use lower long-term targets (V).

## Durability as sustainability (G)
- A finish that lasts 25 years beats a cheap one replaced twice. State the expected life of each proposed finish.
- Hard-wax oiled wood can be spot-repaired; lacquered wood needs full refinishing.
- Porcelain tile, solid wood, stone, quartz and compact laminate last decades; thermofoil and cheap laminate often fail at heat and water.

## Energy in the interior (G)
- LED everywhere; dimming and presence sensors in halls and cupboards.
- Induction instead of gas for new kitchens where the grid and the budget allow; it also improves indoor air.
- Heavy curtains and insulated blinds cut heat loss at night; external shading cuts summer gain.
- Heat-pump dryers and A-rated appliances; water-saving taps (≤ 6 l/min basin, ≤ 8 l/min shower) (V, EU Water Label ranges).
- Thermostatic radiator valves, and do not block radiators with furniture.

## How to present it to the user
Give the sustainable option as one of the concepts or as an "own idea", with the cost difference and the lifetime gain. Never lecture; the user decides.
