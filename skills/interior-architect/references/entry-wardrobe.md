# Entryway, coat storage and wardrobes

Labels: V = cited guideline or manufacturer, R = regulation, G = general practice. Numbers are cm.

## Hanging (V/G)
- Depth of a hanging zone: >= ~46 (V). For hangers facing front across the full width, use >= 50 (G); 60 is the usual size for a wardrobe.
- Hang length (G): short jackets and shirts 100-110; long coats and dresses 150-160. For double hanging, put the upper rod at about the stature of the tallest user, plus 15-20 cm, and keep it within reach.
- Adult hooks at 152-183 high, 25-30 apart (V).

## Shoes (G unless marked)
- Clear height per shelf: ~14 for flats and sneakers, 16-17 for ankle boots, 32-45 for tall boots.
- Width: two pairs side by side need ~46-48 of inner width. Large men's shoes are ~24 wide per pair, small women's ~19.
- Ready-made shoe units are often only 28-35 deep (V, market). A deeper built-in holds more but takes floor space.
- The most common failure is shallow units combined with an underestimate of shoe and bag volume (V). **Count the real pairs.**

## Entry functions to plan for
- Drop-off point for keys, bag and post. A removable tray with a raised edge for wet shoes and umbrellas, on a floor that is easy to clean (V).
- A seat for putting shoes on, 43-48 high and 38-46 deep (V). Guests' slippers, if that is the local custom.
- A full-length mirror and a last check of the outfit. Light the mirror warm, 2700-3000 K, CRI 90+ (G).
- Cleaning tools: a mop, a bucket and an upright vacuum often have no home. Ask about them; they may need a closed, ventilated compartment with a drip tray.

## Door choice
- A hinged door needs the hall width minus the leaf width to stay walkable while it is open (aim for >= 75-80 at that moment). A sliding door saves the swing, but the track costs depth and only one bay can be open at a time.
- Keep each leaf <= 60 wide. A door taller than ~200 needs 3-4 hinges.

## Wardrobes and dressing rooms
- Leave 90 clear in front of wardrobe doors (G). Drawers need their own depth plus ~45 of standing room.
- Put the heavy luggage above the hanging of the taller user. Keep seasonal items in the top band above reach.
- Plan for sun and dust: closed doors protect fabrics. Open systems are cheaper and take less depth, but show clutter.
- Do not block radiators or windows. Leave a gap and add a heat shield on a side panel next to a radiator.
