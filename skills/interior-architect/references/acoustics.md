# Acoustics

Labels: V = cited guideline or manufacturer, G = general practice.

## Three different problems, three different fixes
| Problem | Measure | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Room echoes, speech blurs | **RT60** (seconds for sound to decay 60 dB) | Add **absorption**: rugs, curtains, upholstery, books, acoustic panels |
| Noise passes through a wall, floor or door | **STC** (US) / **Rw** (EU), higher is better | Add **mass, decoupling and airtightness**: double board, resilient bars, insulation in the cavity, solid-core doors with seals |
| Footfall from upstairs | **IIC** (US) / **L'nT,w** (EU, lower is better) | Resilient underlay, floating floor, rugs above |

A heavier door does not fix echo, and an acoustic panel does not stop noise coming through a wall (V).

## Absorption (NRC 0-1, V)
- Hard finishes (plaster, concrete, glass, tile) are NRC ~0.05-0.15: they reflect almost everything.
- Absorbers worth NRC 0.70+: thick fabric panels, wood-wool, felt, cork tiles, heavy lined curtains, deep-pile rugs on underlay.
- In a home, target a living room RT60 of roughly 0.4-0.6 s (G); hard minimalist rooms often reach 1 s or more, which feels harsh and makes the TV hard to hear.
- Rule of thumb (G): treat 15-25 % of the room's surfaces with absorbing material, spread over at least two directions (floor + one wall, or ceiling + one wall).

## Isolation (STC / Rw, V/G)
- A single-stud wall with one board each side is about STC 33-35: normal speech passes through.
- Bedrooms next to living rooms, bathrooms or a combi boiler: aim for STC 50+ / Rw 50+ (G): two boards each side, mineral wool, resilient channel or staggered studs, sealed edges.
- Weak points: doors (hollow-core ~STC 20), gaps under doors, back-to-back sockets, ducts. Seal them first; they cancel a good wall.
- Plumbing noise: wrap waste pipes running through bedroom walls; avoid WC soil pipes beside the bed wall.

## Appliances (G)
- In open kitchens, check the dB rating: a dishwasher at ≤ 44 dB(A) and a hood at ≤ 55 dB(A) on normal setting are quiet enough to talk over.
- Put the washer on a solid floor, not a suspended timber one, or on an anti-vibration pad.

## Design moves
- Bookshelves on a party wall add mass and absorption.
- A soft upholstered headboard wall improves the bedroom.
- Slatted timber ceilings with felt behind are both a look and an absorber.
- In open-plan homes, separate the TV zone from the kitchen with a change of ceiling, a rug and soft seating.
