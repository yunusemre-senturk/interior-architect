# Home office and small spaces

Labels: V = cited guideline, G = general practice. Numbers in cm.

## Home office (V, OSHA Computer Workstations eTool unless marked)
- **Monitor distance:** 50-100 [20-40"] from the eyes. Top of the screen at or slightly below eye level; screen centre 15-20° below the horizontal line of sight. Directly in front, not more than 35° to the side.
- **Desk:** work surface deep enough for ≥ 50 to the screen (≥ 70-80 deep with a monitor arm; 60 deep is tight). Leg clearance under the desk 50-72 high.
- **Desk height:** fixed desks 72-76 (G); better a sit-stand desk, ~62-125 range. Elbows at about 90-120°, shoulders relaxed.
- **Light:** put the desk perpendicular to the window so daylight comes from the side, never behind the screen (glare) or behind the user (reflections) (G). Task light 300-500 lux; tunable-white helps by day (see wellbeing.md).
- **Video calls:** light the face from the front; a plain or curated background; a door that closes and soft surfaces to cut echo (see acoustics.md).
- **Storage:** a closed cabinet that hides the work at the end of the day matters if the office shares a room with living or sleeping (G).
- **Power and data:** 4+ sockets at desk height or in a grommet, a wired network point if possible.

## Small spaces: strategies (G unless marked)
1. **Edit first.** Count what must stay. A small home fails first on the amount of stuff, not on the layout.
2. **Go to the ceiling.** Full-height storage with the top band for seasonal items. A door to the ceiling reads as wall, not furniture.
3. **Thicken one wall.** One deep storage wall (35-60) frees the rest of the room.
4. **Multi-use with real hardware.** Wall bed (vertical needs ~210-220 clear height and ~45 cabinet depth; when open it needs the full bed length in front plus walkway); drop-leaf or extending table; ottoman storage; bench seating with drawers. Name the daily effort: a wall bed that must be made up every night gets abandoned.
5. **Furniture on legs.** Seeing floor under furniture makes rooms read larger; it also lets the robot vacuum pass.
6. **Fewer, larger pieces.** One generous sofa beats two small ones. A large rug makes a room feel larger than a small one.
7. **One material, few seams.** Continuous flooring across rooms, joinery fronts in the wall colour, handle-free fronts.
8. **Sliding and pocket doors** save the swing area (a 80 door swings over ~0.6 m²). A pocket door needs a wall cavity twice the door width with no services in it.
9. **Light and mirrors.** A mirror opposite or beside a window; layered light so corners are lit; pale, matt ceilings.
10. **Zones without walls.** Rugs, a pendant over the table, a change of ceiling, open shelving as a screen.
11. **Hide the kitchen** behind doors in a studio, or choose a single-wall kitchen with full-height storage.
12. **Lofts** need ≥ ~90-100 of headroom above the mattress to sit up, a proper ladder or stair, and a rail. Check the local fire escape rule.

## Rooms that work twice
| Room | Second life |
|---|---|
| Guest room | Office with a wall bed or a sofa bed |
| Dining area | Homework / work table with a nearby closed cabinet |
| Wide hall | Library wall, or a drop-zone and laundry cupboard |
| Bay window | Window seat with drawers, reading nook |
| Under the stairs | Pantry pull-outs, WC (check headroom), desk, pet house |
